CMOS Inverter

VLSI tutorial

Basic structure

A CMOS inverter combines a PMOS transistor in the pull-up network and an NMOS transistor in the pull-down network. Their complementary operation creates an inverted digital output.

Low input

When the input is low, the PMOS tends to conduct while the NMOS is off, pulling the output toward the supply.

High input

When the input is high, the NMOS conducts while the PMOS is off, pulling the output toward ground.

Important characteristics

When studying an inverter, useful quantities include the voltage transfer characteristic, switching threshold, noise margins, propagation delay and power consumption.

Why it matters

The CMOS inverter is a fundamental building block for understanding larger CMOS logic circuits and the trade-offs involved in transistor-level digital design.
